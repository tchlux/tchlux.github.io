Section 01

Adrenal cancer, tumor anatomy, and spread

Cancer

A disease process where abnormal cells grow and survive in ways that escape normal control. Cancer can invade nearby tissues and may spread to distant organs or lymph nodes.

Named systemsoncology, cell biology

Adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC)

A rare cancer arising from the adrenal cortex, the hormone-producing outer layer of the adrenal gland. Also written as adrenal cortical carcinoma, adrenal cortical adenocarcinoma, adrenal carcinoma, or metastatic adrenocortical carcinoma when it has spread.

Named systemsoncology, endocrine system

Metastatic disease / metastatic burden

Cancer that has spread from its original site to other organs or lymph nodes. "Tumor burden" or "metastatic burden" means the overall amount of cancer present.

Named systemsoncology

Right adrenal/hepatic mass

A mass involving the right adrenal region and/or nearby right posterior liver area. Imaging may describe adrenal/hepatic mass, right adrenal mass, dominant liver mass, retroperitoneal mass, or mass effect on nearby structures.

Named systemsoncology, endocrine system, hepatobiliary anatomy

Tumor invasion, vascular invasion, and vessel compression

Terms describing cancer growing into tissues, involving blood vessels, or pressing on critical structures such as the IVC, hepatic veins, renal vein, portal vein, splenic vein, or superior mesenteric vein.

Named systemsoncology, vascular anatomy

Functional adrenal tumor

An adrenal tumor that makes excess hormones, such as cortisol. A cortisol-producing tumor can cause Cushing syndrome and broad metabolic, immune, muscle, blood pressure, and clotting effects.

Named systemsendocrine oncology

Liver metastases / hepatic lesions

Cancer deposits in the liver. Reports may say hepatic metastases, intrahepatic metastases, hepatic lesions, right hepatic lobe lesion, left hepatic lobe lesion, or liver metastatic burden.

Named systemsoncology, hepatobiliary system

Lymphadenopathy / metastatic lymph node

Enlarged lymph nodes. In cancer reports, pericaval, abdominal, or metastatic lymph nodes can reflect regional spread or inflammation.

Named systemslymphatic system, oncology

Debulking / surgical debulking

Surgery intended to reduce tumor volume, such as adrenalectomy or hepatectomy. In this glossary it is a descriptive term, not a treatment recommendation.

Named systemssurgical oncology

Restaging scans and response assessment

Repeat imaging used to judge whether cancer is growing, stable, or shrinking after treatment. RECIST is one formal measurement system used in trials and oncology reports.

Named systemsoncology, radiology

Clinical trial / response rate / median overall survival

Research and outcome terms. Response rate describes the portion of patients whose cancer shrinks by defined criteria; median overall survival is the time point at which half of a study group is alive.

Named systemsoncology research

Section 02

Core anatomy and control systems

Adrenal gland

A paired endocrine organ sitting on top of each kidney. The cortex makes cortisol, aldosterone, and adrenal androgens; the medulla makes catecholamines such as epinephrine and norepinephrine.

Named systemsendocrine system, kidney-adrenal axis, stress response

Adrenal cortex

The outer steroid-producing adrenal layer. The zona glomerulosa mainly makes aldosterone, the zona fasciculata mainly makes cortisol, and the zona reticularis mainly makes adrenal androgen precursors.

Named systemsendocrine system

Adrenal medulla

The inner adrenal tissue that releases catecholamines into blood during sympathetic activation.

Named systemssympathetic nervous system, endocrine system

Right adrenal gland

The adrenal gland above the right kidney. Its close anatomic relationship to the liver and retroperitoneal vessels can make imaging descriptions overlap with hepatic and vascular terms.

Named systemsendocrine system, abdominal anatomy

Retroperitoneum

The deep back part of the abdomen behind the peritoneal cavity. The kidneys, adrenal glands, aorta, IVC, and many lymph nodes sit in or near this space.

Named systemsabdominal anatomy

Renal vein

A vein that drains blood from a kidney into the inferior vena cava. Imaging may mention renal vein patency when checking whether the vessel is open and not blocked or invaded.

Named systemsrenal system, vascular anatomy

Portal vein

The large vein that carries blood from the intestines and spleen toward the liver. Portal vein patency means this vessel is open to blood flow.

Named systemshepatobiliary system, vascular anatomy

Superior mesenteric vein (SMV)

A major abdominal vein that drains blood from much of the small intestine and part of the colon into the portal venous system.

Named systemsgastrointestinal system, vascular anatomy

Splenic vein

The vein that drains blood from the spleen and contributes to formation of the portal vein.

Named systemssplenic system, portal venous anatomy

Distal nephron

The later part of the kidney tubule, including the distal convoluted tubule and collecting system, where fine control of sodium, potassium, hydrogen, and water occurs.

Named systemsrenal system, electrolyte regulation

Collecting duct / collecting duct principal cell

A terminal kidney tubule segment. Principal cells reabsorb sodium and secrete potassium under aldosterone control.

Named systemsrenal system, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system

Suprachiasmatic nucleus

A hypothalamic brain structure that acts as the master circadian clock. It synchronizes sleep-wake timing to light-dark cycles.

Named systemscentral nervous system, circadian system, neuroendocrine regulation

AV node / AV nodal drive

The atrioventricular node is part of the heart's electrical conduction system between atria and ventricles. "AV nodal drive" means the rate and strength of signal conduction through it.

Named systemscardiovascular system, cardiac conduction system

Vascular smooth muscle

The muscle in blood vessel walls that changes vessel diameter. Contraction narrows vessels; relaxation dilates them.

Named systemscardiovascular system, autonomic nervous system

Arteriolar vasodilation / vasoconstriction

Change in small artery diameter. Dilation lowers resistance and usually lowers blood pressure; constriction raises it.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Systemic vascular resistance

The total resistance to blood flow in the systemic circulation. It is one of the main determinants of arterial blood pressure.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Afterload

The pressure or resistance the ventricle must push against to eject blood. Higher afterload makes the heart work harder.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Perfusion

Delivery of blood flow to tissues or organs. Low perfusion can impair kidney function, brain function, and other organ function.

Named systemscardiovascular system, renal system

Section 03

Hormones, endocrine states, and steroid physiology

Cortisol

The major human glucocorticoid, made by the adrenal cortex. It raises blood glucose, alters immune function, affects blood pressure, and helps coordinate stress responses.

Named systemsendocrine system, metabolism, immune system, cardiovascular system

ACTH

Adrenocorticotropic hormone, a pituitary signal that normally tells the adrenal cortex to make cortisol. ACTH-independent Cushing syndrome means cortisol is high without needing excess ACTH drive.

Named systemsendocrine system

Cortisol testing

Tests that measure cortisol in blood or urine, such as serum cortisol, AM cortisol, 24-hour urine cortisol, and free urine cortisol. These terms describe measurement type and timing.

Named systemsendocrine testing

Hypercortisolism / hypercortisolemia

Too much cortisol effect in the body. Hypercortisolemia specifically means high cortisol in blood; hypercortisolism emphasizes the physiologic state.

Named systemsendocrine system, metabolism, immune system, cardiovascular system

Cushing syndrome

The clinical syndrome caused by chronic excess cortisol, whether from tumor, medication, or another cause. Severe Cushing syndrome can affect muscle, infection risk, clotting, glucose, blood pressure, skin, and wound healing.

Named systemsendocrine system, metabolism, immune system, cardiovascular system

Cushingoid appearance

Physical features associated with cortisol excess, including facial plethora, supraclavicular fat pads, and central adiposity.

Named systemsendocrine system

Eucortisolism

A state where cortisol effect is in the intended physiologic range. The term is often used when treatment is trying to lower previously excessive cortisol without causing adrenal insufficiency.

Named systemsendocrine system

Adrenal insufficiency / hypocortisolism

Too little cortisol effect for physiologic needs. Clinical adrenal insufficiency can occur when adrenal hormone production is inadequate or when cortisol-lowering therapy overshoots.

Named systemsendocrine system, critical care

Glucocorticoid replacement / stress-dose steroids

Hydrocortisone or related steroids given to replace cortisol effect. Stress-dose steroids are higher-dose replacement during major physiologic stress.

Named systemsendocrine system, hospital medicine

Fludrocortisone

A mineralocorticoid replacement medication used to provide aldosterone-like sodium-retaining effect when mineralocorticoid activity is inadequate.

Named systemsendocrine system, renal system

Aldosterone

The major mineralocorticoid hormone, made by the adrenal cortex. It tells the kidney to retain sodium and excrete potassium and hydrogen.

Named systemsendocrine system, renal system, blood pressure regulation

Hypoaldosteronism

Too little aldosterone effect, which can contribute to sodium loss, low blood pressure, and high potassium depending on context.

Named systemsendocrine system, renal system

Mineralocorticoid

A steroid hormone or steroid effect that promotes sodium retention and potassium/hydrogen loss, mainly in kidney tubules. Aldosterone is the normal example.

Named systemsendocrine system, renal system

Mineralocorticoid excess physiology

A pattern that behaves like excess aldosterone: hypertension, sodium retention, potassium wasting, and metabolic alkalosis. It can occur from aldosterone itself or from steroid precursors such as DOC.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular system, endocrine system

Mineralocorticoid receptor activation

Activation of the kidney and vascular receptor that mediates aldosterone-like effects. Spironolactone and other MRAs block this receptor.

Named systemsendocrine system, renal system

Glucocorticoid

A steroid hormone class that regulates fuel metabolism, immune tone, and stress response. Cortisol is the main human example.

Named systemsendocrine system, metabolism, immune system

Adrenal androgens / DHEA-S

Adrenal steroid precursors that can contribute to androgen effects or serve as markers of adrenal steroid production. DHEA-S is a sulfated adrenal androgen commonly measured in blood.

Named systemsendocrine system, reproductive endocrinology

Hyperandrogenism / hyperaldosteronism

Hormone excess terms. Hyperandrogenism means excess androgen effect; hyperaldosteronism means excess aldosterone effect.

Named systemsendocrine system

Catecholamines

A family of stress signaling molecules, mainly epinephrine and norepinephrine. They increase heart rate, contractility, blood pressure, and alertness.

Named systemssympathetic nervous system, adrenal medulla, cardiovascular system

Norepinephrine

A catecholamine released mainly from sympathetic nerve endings and also from adrenal medulla. It strongly drives vasoconstriction.

Named systemssympathetic nervous system, cardiovascular system

Adrenergic

Related to catecholamine signaling, especially norepinephrine and epinephrine acting on alpha and beta receptors.

Named systemsautonomic nervous system, cardiovascular system

Pheochromocytoma / metanephrines

Pheochromocytoma is a catecholamine-producing adrenal medulla tumor. Metanephrine and normetanephrine are breakdown products measured when evaluating catecholamine excess.

Named systemsendocrine system, adrenal medulla

Progesterone receptor

A receptor that binds progesterone and related compounds. Off-target binding by some drugs can cause endocrine side effects.

Named systemsendocrine system, reproductive system

Section 04

Receptors, enzymes, transporters, and cell machinery

Receptor

A protein that detects a signal, such as a hormone or neurotransmitter, and starts a cellular response.

Named systemsall signaling biology

Alpha-1 adrenergic receptor

A receptor on vascular smooth muscle and other tissues that responds mainly to norepinephrine by causing contraction, especially vasoconstriction.

Named systemssympathetic nervous system, cardiovascular system

Beta-1 receptor

A receptor concentrated in the heart and juxtaglomerular kidney cells. Activation raises heart rate, contractility, and renin release.

Named systemscardiovascular system, renal system, sympathetic nervous system

Beta-2 receptor

A receptor found in bronchi, vessels, liver, and elsewhere. Activation often causes bronchodilation, metabolic changes, and some vasodilation.

Named systemsrespiratory system, cardiovascular system, metabolism

Mineralocorticoid receptor

An intracellular steroid receptor in kidney and other tissues that mediates aldosterone-like effects.

Named systemsendocrine system, renal system

Insulin receptor tyrosine kinase

The insulin receptor is a membrane protein enzyme. When insulin binds, it activates internal tyrosine kinase activity, starting signaling that promotes glucose uptake and anabolic metabolism.

Named systemsendocrine system, metabolism

MT1 and MT2 receptors

Melatonin receptors in brain and other tissues that help set circadian timing and sleep initiation.

Named systemscentral nervous system, circadian system

L-type calcium channel

A voltage-gated calcium entry channel in muscle and other excitable tissues. In vascular smooth muscle, calcium entry promotes contraction.

Named systemscardiovascular system, muscle physiology

ENaC

Epithelial sodium channel. A sodium entry channel in kidney collecting duct principal cells and a major target of aldosterone signaling.

Named systemsrenal system, electrolyte regulation

GLUT4

An insulin-responsive glucose transporter found mainly in skeletal muscle and fat. Insulin moves it to the cell surface so glucose can enter cells.

Named systemsmetabolism, endocrine system

Na/K-ATPase

The sodium-potassium pump. It uses ATP to move sodium out of cells and potassium into cells, maintaining membrane gradients essential for nerve, muscle, and cell volume control.

Named systemsall excitable tissues, especially nerve, muscle, heart

Cytochrome P450 enzymes / CYP metabolism

A large enzyme family that performs oxidation chemistry. In the adrenal gland, specific P450 enzymes make steroid hormones; in the liver, many CYP enzymes metabolize drugs.

Named systemsendocrine system, hepatic drug metabolism

CYP11A1, CYP17A1, CYP11B1, CYP11B2, CYP3A4

Named P450 enzymes. CYP11A1 starts steroid synthesis, CYP17A1 helps make cortisol and androgens, CYP11B1 makes cortisol, CYP11B2 makes aldosterone, and CYP3A4 is a major liver drug-metabolizing enzyme.

Named systemsadrenal steroidogenesis, liver drug metabolism

11 beta-hydroxylase / 11-beta-hydroxylase inhibition

11 beta-hydroxylase is CYP11B1, the final cortisol-producing enzyme. Blocking it can lower cortisol while causing upstream precursors such as 11-deoxycortisol and DOC to accumulate.

Named systemsendocrine system, adrenal steroidogenesis

Steroidogenesis

The biochemical process of making steroid hormones from cholesterol.

Named systemsendocrine system

Steroidogenesis inhibitor

A medication that lowers steroid hormone production by interfering with adrenal steroid synthesis or adrenal cortical function. Examples in this context include ketoconazole, osilodrostat, metyrapone, mitotane, and etomidate.

Named systemsendocrine pharmacology

11-deoxycortisol

The immediate precursor converted into cortisol by 11 beta-hydroxylase. It can accumulate when that enzyme step is blocked.

Named systemsadrenal steroidogenesis

11-deoxycorticosterone (DOC)

A steroid precursor with meaningful mineralocorticoid activity. If it accumulates, it can raise blood pressure and promote potassium wasting.

Named systemsadrenal steroidogenesis, renal system, cardiovascular system

Precursor steroids upstream of a block

Steroid molecules formed before an inhibited enzymatic step. When a step is blocked, these can accumulate and sometimes have their own biologic activity.

Named systemsendocrine system, pharmacology

Competitive antagonist

A drug that binds a receptor without activating it and blocks the normal signaling molecule from binding. "Competitive" means it competes for the same binding site.

Named systemspharmacology, receptor biology

Agonist

A molecule that binds a receptor and activates it.

Named systemspharmacology, receptor biology

Cofactor

A helper molecule needed by an enzyme to work properly. Magnesium is a common cofactor in ATP-using reactions.

Named systemsbiochemistry, metabolism

Section 05

Metabolism, glucose, and biochemical physiology

Gluconeogenesis

The liver-centered process of making glucose from non-carbohydrate precursors such as amino acids, lactate, and glycerol. Cortisol stimulates it.

Named systemsliver metabolism, endocrine system

Insulin resistance

A state where tissues respond less effectively to insulin, so more insulin is needed to control glucose.

Named systemsendocrine system, metabolism

Glycogen synthesis

Formation of glycogen, the stored polymer form of glucose, mainly in liver and muscle. Insulin promotes it.

Named systemsliver and muscle metabolism

Hepatic glucose output

Glucose released by the liver into blood, largely through glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis.

Named systemsliver metabolism

Hyperglycemia / steroid-induced hyperglycemia

High blood glucose. Cortisol excess and steroid medications can raise glucose by increasing hepatic glucose output and insulin resistance.

Named systemsendocrine system, metabolism

Steroid diabetes physiology

The pattern of high glucose caused or worsened by glucocorticoid effect. It is driven by insulin resistance, increased liver glucose production, and altered muscle/fat glucose uptake.

Named systemsendocrine system, metabolism

HbA1c

A blood test reflecting average blood glucose over roughly the prior 2 to 3 months, with limitations in anemia, transfusion, and rapidly changing illness.

Named systemsendocrine testing, hematology

Blood glucose / POCT glucose / serum glucose

Ways of measuring glucose. POCT glucose is bedside fingerstick or point-of-care testing; serum glucose comes from a lab chemistry sample.

Named systemsendocrine testing, hospital medicine

Insulin regimens

Exogenous insulin is insulin given from outside the body. Basal insulin covers background needs, mealtime insulin covers meals, and correctional or sliding-scale insulin treats high readings.

Named systemsendocrine system, hospital medicine

Hypoglycemia protocol

A hospital process for recognizing and treating low blood glucose. Beta-blockers can mask adrenergic warning symptoms such as palpitations and tremor.

Named systemsendocrine system, autonomic nervous system

DKA / anion gap metabolic acidosis

Diabetic ketoacidosis is a serious insulin-deficient state with high ketones and anion gap metabolic acidosis. The term may appear as a ruled-out diagnosis or risk screen.

Named systemsendocrine system, acid-base physiology

Catabolism / cachexia

Breakdown of body tissue for energy. Cachexia is a cancer- or illness-associated wasting state that may overlap with poor appetite, inflammation, weakness, and edema.

Named systemsmetabolism, oncology, nutrition

Body mass index and weight trend

BMI relates weight to height. Weight trend can be difficult to interpret when edema or fluid weight masks muscle and fat loss.

Named systemsnutrition, fluid balance

Section 06

Acid-base, electrolytes, kidney handling, and fluids

Hypokalemia

Low blood potassium. It can cause weakness, arrhythmias, and worsened cardiac electrical instability. Refractory or labile hypokalemia means potassium is hard to correct or fluctuates.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular system, neuromuscular system

Hyperkalemia

High blood potassium. It can impair cardiac conduction and cause dangerous arrhythmias.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular system

Potassium wasting / renal potassium wasting

Loss of potassium in urine due to kidney handling, often driven by aldosterone-like signaling, low magnesium, or certain drugs.

Named systemsrenal system

Electrolyte derangement / electrolyte monitoring

Electrolyte derangement means clinically important abnormal electrolyte levels. Electrolyte monitoring means repeated lab checks for sodium, potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, calcium, magnesium, or phosphorus.

Named systemsrenal system, laboratory medicine

Potassium repletion / KCl

Replacement of potassium, often as potassium chloride or KCl. It may be oral or IV depending on clinical context.

Named systemselectrolyte regulation, hospital medicine

Magnesium

An electrolyte and enzyme cofactor that affects potassium handling, muscle and nerve function, and cardiac electrical stability. Terms include ionized magnesium, serum magnesium, oral magnesium, and magnesium oxide.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiac electrophysiology, metabolism

Sodium and chloride

Major extracellular electrolytes. Sodium strongly affects extracellular fluid volume; chloride helps balance electrical charge and acid-base physiology.

Named systemsrenal system, fluid balance

Calcium / corrected calcium / ionized calcium

Calcium circulates in protein-bound and free forms. Corrected calcium adjusts total calcium for albumin; ionized calcium measures the biologically active free fraction.

Named systemselectrolyte physiology, bone metabolism

Phosphorus

An electrolyte important for ATP, bone mineral, cell membranes, and muscle function.

Named systemsmetabolism, renal system, bone physiology

Metabolic alkalosis

A blood acid-base disorder with excess base, often due to loss of hydrogen ions or gain/retention of bicarbonate. Mineralocorticoid excess is a common mechanism.

Named systemsrenal system, acid-base physiology

Respiratory alkalosis / mixed alkalosis

Respiratory alkalosis comes from low carbon dioxide due to increased ventilation. Mixed alkalosis means more than one alkalosis process is present.

Named systemsrespiratory system, acid-base physiology

Bicarbonate / HCO3 / CO2

Lab terms used to describe the blood buffering system. Chemistry-panel CO2 usually approximates bicarbonate; HCO3 is bicarbonate on blood gas reporting.

Named systemsacid-base physiology

Anion gap

A calculated chemistry value used to classify metabolic acidosis. A high anion gap can point toward unmeasured acids such as lactate or ketones.

Named systemsacid-base physiology

Hydrogen secretion / H wasting

Kidney excretion of hydrogen ions into urine. Excess hydrogen loss raises blood pH and contributes to metabolic alkalosis.

Named systemsrenal system, acid-base physiology

Sodium reabsorption / sodium retention

Movement of sodium from kidney tubule fluid back into blood. This tends to expand extracellular volume and raise blood pressure.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular system

Volume expansion / Na retention

Increase in extracellular fluid volume, often from sodium retention, which can contribute to edema and hypertension.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular system

Edema / third spacing

Excess fluid accumulation in tissues or spaces outside the bloodstream. It may be described as lower extremity edema, flank edema, subcutaneous edema, ascites, or pleural effusion depending on location.

Named systemscardiovascular system, renal system

Pleural effusion

Fluid in the space around the lung. Small or enlarging bilateral pleural effusions can contribute to breathlessness, low lung volume, or atelectasis.

Named systemspulmonary system, fluid balance

Volume overload / pulmonary edema

Too much fluid in the circulation or tissues. Pulmonary edema means fluid in or around lung tissue that can impair oxygenation.

Named systemscardiovascular system, pulmonary system, renal system

Hydration/diuresis balance

The tension between giving fluid, such as cisplatin hydration, and removing fluid with diuresis when edema, pleural effusions, or pulmonary edema are concerns.

Named systemsoncology, renal system, pulmonary system

Diuresis / loop diuretic / furosemide

Diuresis means increased urine output. Loop diuretics such as furosemide, also called Lasix, promote salt and water excretion.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular system

Creatinine

A blood marker used as an approximate indicator of kidney filtration. A rising creatinine may reflect reduced kidney function or reduced kidney perfusion; low creatinine can reflect low muscle mass.

Named systemsrenal system, muscle mass

eGFR, cystatin C, and renal reserve

eGFR estimates kidney filtration. eGFRcr uses creatinine, eGFRcys uses cystatin C, and eGFRcr-cys combines both; renal reserve refers to kidney capacity under stress.

Named systemsrenal system

BUN

Blood urea nitrogen, a kidney-related chemistry marker affected by hydration, protein metabolism, kidney function, and catabolic state.

Named systemsrenal system, metabolism

Strict intake/output / I/O

Tracking all fluid intake and output. Foley output, urine output, UOP, and oliguria are common urine-output terms.

Named systemsrenal system, hospital medicine

Electrolyte replacement / same-day repletion

Giving potassium, magnesium, or other electrolytes to restore physiologic concentrations, sometimes through same-day lab review and IV or oral repletion.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular system, metabolism

Section 07

Cardiovascular and autonomic terms

Orthostatic hypotension

A drop in blood pressure when standing, due to inadequate compensation for gravity-induced blood pooling. It can cause dizziness or fainting.

Named systemscardiovascular system, autonomic nervous system

Reflex tachycardia

An increase in heart rate caused by a reflex response to falling blood pressure or vasodilation.

Named systemscardiovascular system, autonomic nervous system

Tachycardia / bradycardia

Tachycardia means abnormally fast heart rate; bradycardia means abnormally slow heart rate.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Contractility

The intrinsic force of heart muscle contraction. Increased contractility increases cardiac output and oxygen demand.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Cardiac oxygen demand

How much oxygen the heart muscle requires. It rises with heart rate, contractility, and wall stress.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Sympathetic activation / sympathetic tone

Activity of the sympathetic branch of the autonomic nervous system. It generally raises heart rate, blood pressure, and alertness.

Named systemsautonomic nervous system, cardiovascular system

Vascular catecholamine sensitization

A state where blood vessels respond more strongly than usual to catecholamines. Cortisol can increase this, making blood pressure easier to drive upward.

Named systemsendocrine system, cardiovascular system

Hypertension

High blood pressure. Severe, labile, or secondary hypertension describes high pressure that is extreme, fluctuating, or driven by another condition such as hormone excess.

Named systemscardiovascular system, endocrine system

MAP / mean arterial pressure

A calculated average arterial pressure across the cardiac cycle. It is often used as a perfusion marker in acute care.

Named systemscardiovascular system, critical care

RAAS

The renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, a hormone system that regulates blood pressure, sodium balance, kidney perfusion, and aldosterone production.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular system, endocrine system

ARB, ACE inhibitor, beta-blocker, and nifedipine XL

Blood pressure medication classes or examples. ARBs such as losartan and ACE inhibitors act on RAAS; beta-blockers reduce beta-adrenergic effects; nifedipine XL is a long-acting calcium channel blocker.

Named systemscardiovascular pharmacology

MRA / spironolactone

Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist. Spironolactone blocks aldosterone-like effects at the mineralocorticoid receptor.

Named systemsrenal system, cardiovascular pharmacology

ECG / EKG

Electrocardiogram, a tracing of the heart's electrical activity. It reports intervals such as PR, QRS, and QT/QTc.

Named systemscardiac electrophysiology

QTc / QT prolongation

Corrected QT interval on ECG. QT prolongation means delayed ventricular repolarization and can raise arrhythmia risk, especially with electrolyte abnormalities or QT-active drugs.

Named systemscardiac electrophysiology, pharmacology

PR interval / QRS duration

ECG measures of atrioventricular conduction and ventricular activation timing.

Named systemscardiac conduction system

Premature atrial contractions (PACs)

Early electrical beats arising from the atria. They are common rhythm findings and may appear on ECG or telemetry.

Named systemscardiac electrophysiology

Chronic atrial fibrillation

A persistent irregular atrial rhythm that can affect heart rate, clot risk, and medication choices.

Named systemscardiovascular system, coagulation system

Telemetry

Continuous heart rhythm monitoring, usually in the hospital.

Named systemshospital medicine, cardiac electrophysiology

Palpitations / chest pain

Symptoms that may trigger cardiac evaluation. Palpitations are awareness of heartbeat; chest pain has broad cardiac and noncardiac causes.

Named systemscardiovascular system

BNP / NT-proBNP

Natriuretic peptide blood markers released with cardiac wall stretch. They can support assessment of heart strain or fluid overload.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Troponin / Troponin I

A blood marker of heart muscle injury or stress. It can be monitored with cardiotoxic therapy or acute symptoms.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Transthoracic echocardiogram (TTE)

An ultrasound of the heart done through the chest wall. A bubble study uses injected microbubbles to evaluate shunts.

Named systemscardiovascular imaging

LVEF / ejection fraction

Left ventricular ejection fraction, the percentage of blood ejected from the left ventricle with each beat. It is a common measure of systolic function.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Global longitudinal strain (GLS)

An echocardiographic measure of heart muscle deformation. It can detect subtle changes in cardiac function before ejection fraction falls.

Named systemscardio-oncology, cardiovascular imaging

Right-to-left shunt / shunt physiology

Blood crossing from the right side to the left side of circulation without normal lung oxygenation, or blood bypassing ventilated lung units.

Named systemscardiovascular system, pulmonary system

Septal wall hypokinesis

Reduced motion of the heart's septal wall. It is an echocardiogram finding that may reflect ischemia, conduction effects, strain, or other cardiac conditions.

Named systemscardiovascular imaging

Concentric LV remodeling

Thickening or geometric change of the left ventricle, often related to pressure load such as hypertension.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Biventricular systolic function

How well both the right and left ventricles contract.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Valvular dysfunction

Abnormal valve narrowing or leaking.

Named systemscardiovascular system

Trivial pericardial effusion

A very small amount of fluid around the heart. "Trivial" indicates minimal size on imaging.

Named systemscardiovascular system

IVC / inferior vena cava

The large vein carrying blood from the lower body to the heart. Tumor, liver disease, or volume status can affect its appearance or compression.

Named systemsvascular anatomy, cardiovascular system

Cardiomegaly / cardiomediastinal silhouette

Cardiomegaly means enlarged heart. The cardiomediastinal silhouette is the combined heart and central chest outline seen on chest X-ray.

Named systemscardiovascular imaging

Cardio-oncology

Care focused on heart risks from cancer and cancer treatment. It includes monitoring for anthracycline cardiotoxicity, doxorubicin cumulative dose, biomarkers, echocardiography, and cardioprotective medication.

Named systemsoncology, cardiovascular system

Dexrazoxane

A cardioprotective medication sometimes discussed with anthracycline exposure because it can reduce doxorubicin-related heart injury risk in selected oncology contexts.

Named systemscardio-oncology, oncology pharmacology

Hemodynamic stability / shock

Hemodynamic stability means blood pressure and circulation are adequate. Hemodynamic instability or shock means circulation is failing or at risk of failing.

Named systemscardiovascular system, critical care

Section 08

Respiratory failure, oxygen, and lung mechanics

Acute hypoxemic respiratory failure (AHRF)

A sudden inability to maintain adequate blood oxygen. Also written as acute hypoxic respiratory failure.

Named systemspulmonary system, critical care

Hypoxemia / hypoxia / desaturation

Hypoxemia means low oxygen in blood; hypoxia means inadequate oxygen at the tissue level; desaturation means a drop in measured oxygen saturation.

Named systemspulmonary system, oxygen transport

SpO2 / oxygen saturation

The percentage of hemoglobin binding sites carrying oxygen, usually estimated by pulse oximetry.

Named systemspulmonary system, blood oxygen transport

Oxygen delivery devices

Nasal cannula and low-flow nasal cannula provide lower oxygen flows; high-flow nasal cannula delivers heated humidified high flow; a non-rebreather mask provides high oxygen concentration.

Named systemsrespiratory therapy

FiO2 / flow rate

FiO2 is the fraction of inspired oxygen. Flow rate is the amount of gas delivered per minute.

Named systemsrespiratory therapy

Oxygen weaning / home oxygen qualification

Oxygen weaning means reducing supplemental oxygen as tolerated. Home oxygen qualification or walk testing documents oxygen need with rest or exertion.

Named systemspulmonary system, discharge planning

Respiratory rate / tachypnea / increased work of breathing

Breathing frequency and effort terms. Tachypnea means fast breathing; increased work of breathing means visible or physiologic effort to ventilate.

Named systemspulmonary system

Dyspnea / shortness of breath / dyspnea on exertion

Subjective breathlessness. Dyspnea on exertion means breathlessness with activity.

Named systemspulmonary system, cardiovascular system

Orthopnea / positional desaturation

Orthopnea is breathlessness when lying flat. Positional desaturation means oxygen level changes with body position.

Named systemspulmonary system, cardiovascular system
