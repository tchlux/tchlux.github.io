## Rationales

### Q1

Other-state discipline is reported on renewal or within 90 days, whichever is sooner. With renewal due April 15, renewal is earlier than 90 days from March 3.

### Q2

Electronic reporting with confirmation eliminates the need for an additional oral or written child-abuse report, but the reporter still must notify the person in charge or designated agent and separately report abuse-related death to the coroner or medical examiner of the county where the injuries were sustained.

### Q3

The under-14-day aggregate exception removes the notification requirement for the first period only. The later temporary-assignment period requires written notification and written permission before practice begins.

### Q4

Temporary assignment is up to 6 months, with only one extension of up to 6 more months.

### Q5

The treatment-capacity exemption remains. Refusal to enter the required Board agreement disqualifies the colleague from the impaired-professional program and activates an immediate investigation and disciplinary proceeding.

### Q6

After 2 years, the conduct is not automatically permitted; the psychologist bears the burden of showing no exploitation. Consent is not a defense, and the impaired-professional diversion route is unavailable when the Board proceeds under the sexual-intimacy sections.

### Q7

The misdemeanor must be reported within 30 days or on renewal, whichever is sooner. The other-state final disciplinary order must be reported within 90 days of notice or on renewal, whichever is sooner.

### Q8

On active suspension, the licensee must return the license and provide written notice within 30 days to current clients/patients and supervisees seeking licensure, including sanction, effective date and length, nature of violation, and assistance with alternative resources and record transfer.
