# PA Psychology Law Hard Questions Key

## Q1

The psychologist must report the other-state disciplinary action to the Board on the biennial renewal application or within 90 days, whichever is sooner. Once Pennsylvania actively suspends the license, the psychologist must return the suspended license to the Board and, within 30 days of receiving notice of the Pennsylvania discipline, notify all current clients/patients and all individuals obtaining supervision for licensure from the psychologist. That notice must state the sanction, the effective date and length, the nature of the violation, and that the psychologist will assist with alternative professional resources and transfer of records.

Sources: `Section 8.1`, `Section 17`, `§ 41.91(b)`, `§ 41.92`

## Q2

The psychologist must report suspected abuse-related death to the coroner or medical examiner of the county where the injuries were sustained, not the county where death occurred, because the child was transported for treatment. The child-abuse reporting rules override Ethical Principle 5 and other professional confidentiality rules. Privileged communications do not excuse the duty to report, and releasing information necessary to complete the report does not violate the Mental Health Procedures Act.

Sources: `§ 41.73`, `§ 41.75`

## Q3

Because more than 2 years have passed, the conduct is not automatically barred, but the psychologist bears the burden of proving there was no exploitation in light of the listed seven factors. Client consent is not a defense. If the Board takes disciplinary or corrective action for conduct prohibited by `§§ 41.81—41.83`, the psychologist is not eligible for placement in an impaired-professional program instead of discipline.

Sources: `§ 41.83`, `§ 41.84(a), (c)`, `§ 41.85`

## Q4

For the 13-day aggregate period, the psychologist is exempt from the temporary-assignment notification requirement. For the later 7-month period, the psychologist may practice on temporary assignment only after written notification to the Board and receipt of written temporary permission to practice. Temporary assignment lasts up to 6 months, with no more than one extension of up to 6 additional months. A provisional endorsement license may be issued while remaining endorsement requirements are being completed; it lasts up to 1 year, with a possible written-extension of no longer than 1 additional year for good cause. After expiration or termination, the person may reapply for endorsement, but may not receive a second provisional endorsement license.

Sources: `§ 41.52(c)`, `§ 41.52b`

## Q6

The employee must sign reports, records, and communications the employee prepares. Any written communication going outside the employment setting must be signed by the employee and countersigned as `reviewed and approved by` the supervisor. Billing and public announcements must disclose the employee's unlicensed supervised status. Clients/patients must be told the employee is supervised and given specific information about qualifications and functions. The employing psychologist retains full and ultimate professional responsibility for client welfare and services, even if supervisory tasks are assigned to another licensed psychologist.

Sources: `Section 3(12)`, `§ 41.58(b), (c)(6)-(8), (d)`

## Q7

No, a person acting in a treatment capacity to an impaired professional in an approved treatment program is exempt from the mandatory-reporting requirement in the impaired-professional subsection. But refusal to enter the Board agreement disqualifies the colleague from the impaired-professional program and triggers an immediate Board investigation and disciplinary proceeding.

Sources: `Section 18(c)`, `Section 18(f)`

## Q8

Case A uses temporary suspension for immediate and clear danger under `Section 8(d)`. It can be imposed without a prior hearing but with due notice and allegations. A preliminary hearing to determine a prima facie case must occur within 30 days, the licensee has participation rights there, and the temporary suspension cannot last more than 180 days. The refusal to submit to a compelled examination under `Section 8(a)(8)` is treated as an admission of the allegations unless circumstances beyond the psychologist's control caused the refusal. Case B uses automatic suspension under `Section 8(e)` because of the controlled-substance felony conviction. That automatic suspension is not stayed pending appeal.

Sources: `Section 8(a)(8)`, `Section 8(d)`, `Section 8(e)`

## Q9

The misdemeanor conviction must be reported within 30 days of the conviction or on the biennial renewal application, whichever is sooner. The other-state final disciplinary order must be reported within 90 days of receiving notice of that disciplinary action or on the biennial renewal application, whichever is sooner.

Sources: `§ 41.91(a)-(b)`

## Q10

The reporter must still notify the person in charge of the institution, school, facility, or agency, or that person's designated agent, immediately after making the report. But once an electronic report is made and confirmation of receipt is obtained, no additional oral or written report is required.

Sources: `§ 41.71(b)-(c)`
