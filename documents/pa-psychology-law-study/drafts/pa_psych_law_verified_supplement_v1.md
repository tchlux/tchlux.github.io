# PA Psychology Law Verified Supplement V1

For each question, choose all options that are fully supported by the Pennsylvania source material.

## Q1

Choose all fully supported statements about child-abuse reporting and confidentiality.

A. The reporting requirements of `§§ 41.71-41.73` take precedence over Ethical Principle 5 and any other ethical principle or professional standard that might otherwise apply to psychologists.  
B. Privileged communications between a mandated reporter and a patient/client do not apply to a situation involving child abuse and do not relieve the mandated reporter of the duty to make a report of suspected child abuse.  
C. A mandated reporter who releases information necessary to complete a report of suspected child abuse violates the Mental Health Procedures Act unless a court first authorizes the disclosure.  
D. A mandated reporter who makes a report of suspected child abuse does not violate the Mental Health Procedures Act by releasing information necessary to complete the report.  

## Q2

Choose all fully supported statements about the good-faith protections tied to child-abuse reporting.

A. A mandated reporter who participates in good faith in making a suspected-child-abuse report, making a referral for general protective services, cooperating or consulting with an investigation, testifying in a related proceeding, or engaging in another authorized action under Chapter 63 has immunity from civil and criminal liability that might otherwise result.  
B. The good-faith presumption applies in civil proceedings only, not criminal proceedings or Board disciplinary proceedings.  
C. The Board will uphold the same good-faith presumption in a disciplinary proceeding that might result by reason of a licensed psychologist’s actions under `§§ 41.71-41.73`.  
D. The immunity is limited to the initial report itself and does not extend to later consultation, testimony, or other authorized conduct.  

## Q3

Choose all fully supported statements about exemptions from the child-abuse training or continuing-education requirements.

A. The Board will grant an exemption if the applicant or licensee proves only that children are not part of the applicant’s or licensee’s practice.  
B. For licensure renewal, previously completed equivalent training counts toward an exemption only if it equals or exceeds the required amount and was completed during the relevant biennial renewal period.  
C. An exemption granted under `§ 41.77(c)` applies only for the biennial renewal period for which the exemption is requested.  
D. If an exemption request is denied, the Board may still renew the license immediately and then request later documentation if needed.
